About the Twin Blockchain

The Twin Blockchain is a small family of blockchains built and kept by one house. Four coin chains — TWOPI, KEEL, AGORA and TESSERA — each keep their own ledger, and one hub ties them together. This website is the family's public front door: it publishes the chains' own records, refreshed about every five minutes, so anyone can watch the family run.

Why "Twin"?

The family began as a pair — a chain and its mirror — and grew into four coin chains joined at one hub. Every coin chain anchors a fingerprint of itself into the hub, so the whole family's history can be checked as one piece. The four were verified together, end to end, as a single combination on October 25, 2026.

Who keeps it

The family is kept by Mira Moon — builder and keeper of the Twin Blockchain — under the direction of the house that commissioned it. A standing governing body watches the chains and this website around the clock: the residents producing, the anchors standing, the site answering, the published data fresh. When something stalls, it is written down as an alert the day it happens — never quietly smoothed over.

What this site can and cannot do

You can: watch every chain live, read each coin's fixed supply and founding accounts, and search every block the family has ever produced.

You cannot: buy, sell, trade, stake or hold these coins through this site, and there is no account to create. The coins are not for sale, they have no market price, and nothing here is financial advice, an offer, or a promise of anything. If a site or a person offers to sell you TWOPI, KEEL, AGORA or TESSERA, it is not us.

Interaction on this site means exploration — reading the family's public record for yourself. Anything beyond that would be a separate, announced step, and it would be announced here first.

How to check a number yourself

  1. Note the height shown for a chain on the home page, and the exact update time printed above it.
  2. Open the explorer and search that height — the block, its time and its fingerprint should match what the coin page shows.
  3. Read the words you are unsure of in the glossary. If anything still disagrees, that is a correction worth reporting below.

Spot an error? Tell us

If a number, name or explanation on this site looks wrong, that is worth reporting — this site exists so strangers can check the record, and a correction from a reader is a gift. Reach Mira through any of the public channels listed above (X, Instagram, Threads, Facebook or YouTube) and say which page and what looks wrong. Corrections are made at the source file, re-verified on the live page, and noted in the site's improvements log, which its governing body keeps.

Your privacy on this site

This site collects nothing about you: there are no accounts, no forms, no cookies set by this site, and no trackers. Your browser fetches the pages and the chains' published numbers, and that is the whole exchange. What the hosting provider (GitHub Pages) logs on its side is governed by GitHub's own privacy statement.

How the numbers get here

Each chain writes its own records as it runs, and those records are public: this site shows them, refreshed about every five minutes — the numbers come from the chains themselves, never from anything private and never from any keys. Every page shows when its data was published; if an update is ever late, the page says so plainly instead of pretending to be live.
