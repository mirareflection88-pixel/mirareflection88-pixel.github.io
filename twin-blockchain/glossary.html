The words, in plain language

This site uses a handful of blockchain words. Here is each one in ordinary English — no prior knowledge assumed.

Jump to a word: Block · Hash · Height · Validator · Anchor · Hub · Coin chain · Snapshot · Supply · Ledger · Transaction · First block · Combination · Block time · Burn · Emission · Fee · Address · Wallet · Public record · Explorer

Block
One sealed page of a chain's ledger. Each block records what happened in its moment, and once sealed it is not rewritten.
Hash (fingerprint)
A short string of characters made from everything in a block. Change one letter anywhere in the block and the fingerprint no longer matches — that is how anyone can check the record.
Height
How many blocks a chain has produced, counted from its first block. A chain's height is its age in blocks: higher means it has been running longer.
Validator
The account that produces a chain's blocks — the keeper that seals each new page of the ledger in turn.
Anchor
A fingerprint of one chain written into another chain (here, into the family hub), so the first chain's history can later be checked against a record it cannot quietly change itself.
Hub
The family's tying-together chain. Each coin chain anchors into the hub, which is what makes four chains one combination.
Coin chain (spoke)
One of the four chains that carries a coin — TWOPI, KEEL, AGORA or TESSERA. Each keeps its own ledger and its own blocks.
Snapshot
The published copy of the chains' own records that this site reads. A small publisher posts a fresh snapshot about every five minutes, and every page shows the snapshot's time, so you always know how current the numbers in front of you are.
Supply / cap
How much of a coin exists in total. For every coin in this family the amount is fixed at a cap set when the coin was created — no more can ever be made.
Ledger
The full record a chain keeps — every block, in order, from the first one to the newest. A chain's ledger is its history book, and it is written so it cannot be quietly rewritten afterwards.
Transaction
One recorded action inside a block — for example, the anchor submissions that joined this family. A block can hold one transaction, many, or none.
First block (genesis)
The very first block of a chain, created when the chain launched. Every later block is counted from it — that count is the chain's height.
Combination
This family's joining, verified end to end on October 25, 2026: the four coin chains anchored into one hub, so the whole family can be checked as one piece.
Block time
The moment a block was sealed, as recorded in the block itself. Times on this site are shown in your local timezone with the zone named.
Burn
Coins permanently removed from circulation. TWOPI stands a hair under its cap because three anchor fees were burned when the family combined.
Emission
Coins released slowly over time by design. AGORA is the family's only emitting coin: its rewards account pays out a small amount with every block, and the total never passes its fixed cap.
Public record
The published snapshot and block index anyone can fetch and check — the same files this site reads. If this page and the public record ever disagreed, the record would win and the page would be corrected.
Fee
The small charge a chain collects for recording an action, such as an anchor submission. In this family, fees are burned — permanently removed from circulation — rather than paid out. Looking at this site costs nothing: there is no fee for reading the public record.
Address
The string of characters that identifies one account on a chain — where its balance is kept and where its actions are recorded. Each coin page prints its founding accounts' addresses in full.
Wallet
A tool that holds the keys to an account and signs actions for it. This site is not a wallet: it holds no keys, takes no signatures, and cannot move any coin.
Explorer
This site's search tool: it lets anyone look up any block by its number (height) or its fingerprint (hash), and check the family's record for themselves.
