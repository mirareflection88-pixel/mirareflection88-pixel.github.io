GeoMiner

Work the tiles. Claim the land.

State: being built — not published yet

GeoMiner is a mining game played on a map. You work survey tiles — probe, drill, extract — and earn points for real work done on the tile in front of you. Points buy land: Survey Estates, parcels of digital ground that pay their rent back in points. The game is being built on the Twin Blockchain family, and its numbers read the family's real chains.

How it plays

Work the tile

Three kinds of work, in rising depth: Probe pays 2 points, Drill pays 6, and Extract pays 15 on a fresh tile. Work a tile again inside the hour and it pays a 1-point floor — the game rewards moving and surveying, not sitting on one spot. There is no luck in it anywhere: every number is printed on the parcel before you commit.

Earn, honestly capped

Mining pays up to 300 points a day. Starter quests pay one-time bonuses of +10, +15, +25 and +50 as you learn the ground. Points are the game's own ledger — earned by work, never bought.

Claim land

Land is claimed by presence: work a tile well, then claim its parcel. Your first parcel is free; after that a parcel costs 1,000 points times its tier. You can hold up to 10 parcels, and claim up to 3 a day.

Survey Estates — the land

Every site on the map holds 24 parcels — 360 in the first slice of the world. A parcel's tier is set by its ground and printed on its face; the tier weights both its price and its share of the rent:

GroundTier weight
Urban1.0
Coastal1.2
Arid1.2
Alpine1.4
Volcanic1.6

Rent flows from a published land budget, shared between parcel owners in proportion to tier weight — a Volcanic parcel earns 1.6 shares to an Urban parcel's 1. Rent lands as ordinary points, so land funds more surveying and more land.

Tied to the real chains

GeoMiner is not a picture of a blockchain game — its bridge to the family is built and proven. A read-only connection reads the chains' own published records, so the game's ground is the family's ground:

  • KEEL, the steady chain, stands behind the land: its stewards hold 40,000,000 KEEL of record.
  • AGORA, the commons, set aside 175,000,000 in its rewards account at the family's founding, and pays out a little with every block the chain produces — the commons grows as the chain runs.

Heights, balances and the family's live state are on the Twin Blockchain site, which publishes them about every five minutes.
